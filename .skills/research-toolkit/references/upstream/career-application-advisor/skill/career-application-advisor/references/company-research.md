# Employer and role research

## Research questions

Answer two separate questions:

1. Is the company real, viable, governable, and reasonably stable?
2. Is this specific role valuable for this job seeker now?

## Research dimensions

### Product and customers

Identify what the company sells, who pays, whether the need is real and recurring, the product's maturity, and whether the target business line is core, growth, or peripheral.

### Business model

Assess acquisition, sales, delivery, collection, repeat revenue, scalability, and dependence on subsidies, financing, a single channel, or a single customer.

### Operating quality

Use revenue, margin, cash flow, users, orders, retention, market share, or credible proxies. Mark unavailable data as unavailable. Never substitute funding for operating quality.

### Competitive advantage

Assess product, technology, data, intellectual property, supply chain, cost, delivery, channel, brand, community, customer relationships, and organizational capability. Test whether advantages are durable and difficult to copy.

### Strategy and execution

Compare stated strategy with actual products, hiring, investment, launches, retrenchment, and historical execution.

### Industry position

Assess market position, customer recognition, ecosystem role, talent-market reputation, and how much future resume value the brand can provide.

### Leadership and governance

Review founders, controlling parties, leadership track record, executive turnover, litigation, regulatory exposure, related-party issues, and organizational stability.

### Capital and financing

Check investors, financing dates, capital actions, pledges, and whether capital has translated into product, distribution, and organizational capability.

### Hiring and organization

Assess hiring or layoff trends, role repetition, reporting line, decision authority, budget, resources, success metrics, vacancy reason, and whether the title matches the work.

## Source hierarchy

1. Primary: official products, filings, regulators, registries, courts, audited reports, and formal announcements.
2. Strong secondary: reputable media, established databases, investor disclosures, and named interviews.
3. Contextual: job boards, employee reviews, social media, communities, and channel observations.
4. Unverified: recruiter, interviewer, or candidate statements.

Use at least two independent sources for decisive claims when practical. Keep links and information dates near the claims they support.

## Research depth

- Quick check: confirm entity, product, role, timeline, and obvious red flags.
- Standard review: assess all material dimensions for an unfamiliar employer or meaningful move.
- Deep diligence: expand legal, financial, governance, and leadership research for executive roles, major compensation decisions, equity-heavy offers, or clear conflicts.

## Red flags

Investigate rather than automatically reject:

- legal entity or brand mismatch;
- vague product, customer, or revenue logic;
- stale funding presented as recent momentum;
- repeated hiring for the same role without explanation;
- title inflation with weak authority or resources;
- inconsistent headcount, business scale, and financing claims;
- high executive turnover or sudden strategic pivots;
- compensation heavily deferred, conditional, or equity-based without clear terms;
- pressure to pay, provide sensitive credentials, or accept before receiving written terms.

## Interview verification questions

Ask what would change the conclusion:

- Why is the role open now?
- What outcome defines success in six and twelve months?
- Who owns the budget and final decisions?
- What team, data, systems, and cross-functional support exist?
- Which business line funds the team and how is it prioritized?
- What happened to the previous role holder?
- Which offer terms are guaranteed, discretionary, deferred, or conditional?
