# Resume review and tailoring

## Review sequence

1. Read every page and identify the intended role, seniority, industry, location, and language.
2. Check whether the first third of the document communicates a coherent professional identity.
3. Map each important claim to evidence: scope, personal action, authority, difficulty, outcome, and measurement basis.
4. Check chronology, overlapping dates, unexplained transitions, degree dates, titles, and inconsistent metrics.
5. Remove repetition, generic self-evaluation, irrelevant early projects, and tool lists that do not support the target role.
6. Evaluate scanability and applicant-tracking compatibility without sacrificing readability.

## Evidence ladder

Prefer, in order:

1. Verified outcome with scale and personal contribution.
2. Quantified outcome with a clear measurement basis.
3. Specific ownership and scope without a measurable outcome.
4. Generic responsibility.
5. Unsupported adjective or self-rating.

Ask for more information when a high-value claim remains at levels 4 or 5.

## Missing-information questions

Ask only questions that can improve selection decisions. Prioritize:

- What business problem existed?
- What was the scale: revenue, users, budget, team, geography, volume, or time?
- What did the candidate personally decide, design, execute, or influence?
- What was inherited from the team, platform, process, or manager?
- What changed, compared with what baseline, over what period?
- How was the result measured and who accepted it?
- What constraint or difficulty made the work meaningful?
- Which target-role requirement does this evidence support?

If exact numbers are confidential, request a percentage, range, indexed change, or qualitative result that remains truthful.

## JD matching

Translate the JD into five buckets:

1. Mission: the problem the hire is expected to solve.
2. Must-have evidence: requirements likely used for screening.
3. Preferred evidence: useful differentiators.
4. Risk signals: gaps, overqualification, instability, location, language, or domain mismatch.
5. Interview unknowns: authority, resources, reporting line, success metrics, and reasons for hiring.

Do not add keywords the candidate cannot defend. Do not disguise a real capability gap as a wording problem.

## Rewriting guidance

- Lead with role-relevant evidence, not a complete autobiography.
- Use direct verbs that accurately match ownership: supported, executed, owned, led, designed, approved, or decided.
- Keep bullets focused on one contribution or decision.
- Put the most decisive evidence first under each role.
- Use consistent dates, punctuation, tense, and number formatting.
- Keep education and older projects proportional to their current decision value.
- Create distinct variants for materially different job families.

## Final document QA

Check contact details, clipping, broken glyphs, isolated headings, blank pages, date alignment, legibility, links, file naming, selectable PDF text, and visual consistency between editable and PDF versions.
