# Interview Narratives

## Procedure

1. Start from the role's business problems and the evidence ledger, not generic interview questions.
2. For “tell me about yourself,” use: professional logline → two role-relevant proof points → why this is the logical next step. Keep biography secondary.
3. Choose a structure by question:
   - STAR for clear chronology;
   - CAR or PAR for a compact problem story;
   - HERO for senior work: headline outcome, effect, rationale/trade-offs, operations.
4. Lead with the answer or outcome, then provide only enough context for the listener to evaluate it.
5. Explain decisions, alternatives, collaboration, and trade-offs—not only tasks.
6. Practice aloud and time the answer. Aim for roughly 60–90 seconds unless the interview format calls for more detail.
7. Prepare clarifying questions using who, what, where, when, why, and how when the prompt is ambiguous.
8. End with one memorable takeaway and invite deeper questions.

## Guardrails

Stay relevant and professional without inventing a persona. Never memorize generated claims that do not map to evidence. Calm structure is the goal; scripted perfection is not.
