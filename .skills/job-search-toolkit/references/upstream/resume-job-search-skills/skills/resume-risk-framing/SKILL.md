# Risk Framing

## Procedure

1. Identify the ambiguity and what a reasonable reviewer cannot infer from the page.
2. Decide whether to explain it on the resume, LinkedIn, application field, or only in an interview. Explain only when clarity outweighs extra attention.
3. Use a short factual line: event → neutral context → current readiness or forward direction.
4. For a title mismatch, retain the official title in the ledger and add a truthful functional clarifier in parentheses when needed.
5. For a gap, mention real work, study, caregiving, or personal leave at the level of detail the candidate chooses. Never invent consulting or a company.
6. For seniority or overqualification, emphasize current relevance, rapid contribution, judgment, adaptability, and desired scope—not years alone.
7. Check resume and LinkedIn for contradictions before submission.

## Hard boundaries

Do not falsify education, employment dates, termination status, credentials, language fluency, reporting lines, management scope, or skills. Do not advise hiding information required by an application or background check. Personal health, family status, politics, and salary history stay out unless the candidate intentionally chooses disclosure and it is necessary.
